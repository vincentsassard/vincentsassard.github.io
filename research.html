Research

Linking the deep Earth to the surface.

Interior Alaska

Seismic Imaging of the Alaskan Subduction System

Using full-wave ambient noise tomography applied to more than 500 seismic stations across Alaska and the Yukon Territory, I construct a three-dimensional shear wave velocity model. In this study, I focus on northeastern Alaska and the Yukon Territory, in particular the Yukon Flats Basin. I find that the lithosphere beneath the Yukon Flats Basin is particularly thin due to transtension and it is intruded by the asthenosphere along the Tintina Fault and the Porcupine Shear Zone whose damaged rocks facilitate magma transport.

Alaska Peninsula

Seismic Imaging of the Alaskan Subduction System

Using full-wave ambient noise tomography applied to the Alaska Amphibious Community Seismic Experiment (AACSE), I construct a three-dimensional shear wave velocity model of the Alaska Peninsula. Key findings include evidence of slab tearing along a subducted oceanic plate joint beneath the Alaska Peninsula (published in Nature Geoscience, 2025).

Cascadia

Segmentation of the Cascadia Subduction Zone

My MSc thesis at Universiteit Utrecht investigated the controls on lateral segmentation along Cascadia, comparing competing hypotheses involving sub-slab density anomalies and variations in slab age. This work combined numerical geodynamic modeling with seismological constraints, and contributed to ongoing discussion about the relationship between slab structure, trench geometry, and overlying deformation.

Galápagos

Upper Mantle Dynamics at the Galápagos Hotspot

As part of the Marine IGUANA experiment aboard the R/V Sally Ride (2024), I participated in the recovery of Ocean Bottom Seismometers around the Galápagos hotspot and spreading center. This multi-disciplinary cruise involved seismic data analysis, multi-beam bathymetry correction, and deployment of oceanographic instruments. I am co-author on two AGU 2025 presentations analyzing shear-wave splitting and upper mantle flow in this setting.

DAS

Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS)

During a research internship at Penn State (2025), I developed a workflow to process Distributed Acoustic Sensing data for seismic monitoring of the Susquehanna Shale Hills Critical Zone Observatory. DAS leverages fiber-optic cables as dense, continuous seismic arrays, opening new possibilities for environmental monitoring and near-surface characterization.

Anisotropy

Seismic Anisotropy & Mantle Flow

As an undergraduate researcher at the University of Oregon, I analyzed shear-wave splitting from onshore and offshore stations of the Alaska Amphibious Community Seismic Experiment to test mantle anisotropy models in the Alaskan subduction zone. This work, supervised by Dr. Miles Bodmer, resulted in two AGU poster presentations and laid the foundation for my subsequent Ph.D. research on lithospheric structure.

Field

Field Geology & Sample Collection

Beyond geophysics, I have participated in field campaigns including plutonic sample collection in the remote Northern Cascades (2023) and seismometer network deployment/retrieval in Northern California for the SCENTAR project. These experiences ground my quantitative seismological work in direct observation of the geological record.
